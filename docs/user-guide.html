GRV Supply Chain Control Tower

Complete user guide for the control tower — availability, manufacturing, the weekly review, S&OP and BOM cost analysis — for both regular users and administrators.

Version 3.0 Updated Aug 2026 grivelsc.github.io/GRV-Item-Availability/
Introduction

What is the GRV Supply Chain Control Tower?

The GRV Supply Chain Control Tower is a live web dashboard that shows how many units of each finished-goods SKU Grivel can currently ship — and when more will become available. It pulls data from NetSuite (the company's ERP system) and computes availability across the full bill of materials for every product.

The dashboard covers two locations:

  • VERRAYES — the manufacturing facility in Italy (full BOM-aware availability).
  • USA-3PL — the distribution warehouse in the USA (finished goods only).

It is intended for use by sales, operations, and customer service teams who need to answer questions like: "Can we fulfil this order? When can we ship? What's holding us back?"

Data freshness

Data is refreshed manually. The timestamp in the top-right corner shows when the data was last updated. If you are unsure of freshness, contact the person responsible for running weekly refreshes.

Accessing the Dashboard

The dashboard is hosted publicly at:

https://grivelsc.github.io/GRV-Item-Availability/

No login is required. The link is also bookmarked in the company SharePoint intranet. It works in any modern browser — Chrome, Firefox, Safari, or Edge.

Each row represents a moment where something changes — a PO arrives, or a batch of SO ship dates passes. Steps where nothing changes are removed to keep the view compact. If the Net Available column stays at 0 throughout, the item has no current or planned supply.

The download always exports all items regardless of active filters, and always in units — the Units/Value €$ toggle never affects the file. Use your spreadsheet app to apply additional filtering or to value the quantities after download.

The Other Tabs

WBR · Weekly Business Review

The WBR tab turns whatever the filter bar is currently showing into a ready-to-read weekly review: what moved since last week, and how the portfolio recovers over the next twelve months.

What "week over week" actually compares against

The comparison anchor is the opening position of the previous refreshed week — not exactly seven days ago. Each refresh keeps the earliest snapshot of the current week; when a new week's first refresh runs, that snapshot is promoted to the baseline. If a week is skipped entirely, the baseline is the last week that was actually refreshed, and the date shown next to WoW vs in the header tells you which.

Until the first refresh of the following week runs, you will see "Baseline forming — WoW metrics will appear after the first refresh of next week…". That is expected on a newly deployed dashboard.

The baseline is filtered with exactly the same rules as the live view, so the deltas are always like-for-like with your current location, lifecycle, class, status and search selection.

KPI strip

Six cards — Total SKUs, In Stock, Committed, Incoming, Out of Stock, and Committable value — each with a delta chip underneath showing the change in count and in percentage points of the portfolio. Green means movement in the good direction, red bad, grey neutral.

The four sections

  • WoW Commentary — a written narrative generated from the numbers. It covers the in-stock rate change, the SKUs that entered and left stock, which classes drove the move, and a decomposition splitting the change into a rate effect (availability genuinely changed) and a mix effect (the portfolio composition shifted). It closes with a projection of the in-stock rate at month 3 and month 12.
  • Incoming Wave — Total — twelve forward months, each split into in stock, incoming and out of stock. This is the recovery profile of the whole selection.
  • Incoming Wave — By Class — the same profile per product class, so you can see which categories recover and which stay flat.
  • WoW Movers — the SKUs that entered stock, went out of stock, swung the most in value, or whose next-availability date slipped by more than a week.

Every section collapses. Everything recalculates when you change a filter.

The Other Tabs

S&OP · Sales & Operations Planning

The S&OP tab looks roughly six months back and twelve forward and asks whether the demand plan holds up against budget and the order book, whether supply and production capacity can meet it, and what inventory position that leaves.

Three sub-views, each a chart plus an expandable class-by-month matrix. Click a class name to collapse or expand its detail rows.

Demand Review

Actuals (split B2B and DTC), forecast, order book and budget on one timeline. Rows per class: forecast, budget, forecast versus budget, implied ASP, order book, and the order book as a percentage of forecast and of budget.

Two details worth knowing. Actuals are net of invoice discounts, allocated pro-rata within each month and channel. And the forecast versus budget row compares actuals to budget for closed months but forecast to budget from the current month forward — the current month is deliberately excluded from the actuals comparison because it is incomplete.

Supply Review

Firm and planned work orders and purchase orders, stacked by month. At Verrayes, in Units mode, a capacity line and a load-versus-capacity row appear once capacity has been uploaded — red above 100%, amber from 85%, green below.

Projected Inventory

The forward position, month by month: projected = previous + supply − demand, where demand is the greater of forecast and order book. Backlog, unscheduled orders and overdue supply all land in the current month. Weeks of cover is the projected quantity divided by the average weekly forecast of the following quarter, coloured against your uploaded cover-target bands.

S&OP needs its own inputs

Budget, production capacity, legacy sales history and inventory cover targets are uploaded through the Admin Panel and stored in sop_config.json, separate from config.json. Without a budget, value mode falls back to a trailing twelve-month realised ASP and the budget lines simply do not appear. Uploads only change your browser copy — you must click ⬇ Download Updated sop_config.json and place the file in C:\GRV-Availability\ for them to survive the next refresh.

The Other Tabs

BOM · Cost Build-up

The BOM tab is the one part of the control tower that is diagnostic rather than operational. It exists to explain what a finished good costs to make and why, not to drive a daily decision.

What "COGS" means here

A deliberately narrow definition: the sum, over every leaf of the bill of materials, of that component's procurement cost multiplied by how much of it goes into one finished good.

It therefore excludes internal labour and overhead, freight and duties, header-level discounts, and scrap or yield allowances. It includes outsourced operations, because a conto-lavoro service appears as its own line inside the parent's bill of materials.

This will not reconcile to an accounting COGS, and it is not meant to. It is a should-cost build-up from purchasing data.

Everything on this tab is Grivel S.r.l. and euros. Intercompany purchases are excluded everywhere, so no transfer price ever leaks into a cost.

Reading the indented bill of materials

The tree opens fully expanded down to the leaves; click any triangle to collapse a branch. Sub-assembly rows carry their own rolled-up cost, so you can read what a branch contributes without expanding it. Qty / FG is the cumulative usage per one finished good — the quantities multiplied all the way down the path — which is what the cost is calculated on.

Three inline flags can appear next to a component:

  • UOM — the item is purchased in a different unit from the one it is stocked in, and no conversion has been configured. Its cost is unreliable until an administrator adds one.
  • CL — a conto-lavoro sub-assembly: it contains an outsourced service line.
  • BUY — the item has a bill of materials but is also purchased directly. A make-versus-buy hybrid worth a look.

Some finished goods are bought in rather than built — packs, bags and traded accessories. They have no bill of materials, so they appear as a single line: the product is its own leaf, costed from its own purchase history.

The Data coverage KPI is the share of the COGS backed by the top two rungs — real transactions inside the window. The Cost source coverage & gaps panel lists every component that fell through to a fallback, ranked by how much money it represents, with the reason. That list is a to-do for procurement data hygiene as much as a caveat.

Unit costs are always derived as amount divided by quantity rather than from the rate field, which means they are net of discounts and converted to euros automatically even on purchases made in another currency.

COGS by category

The split is computed from the leaves only. Three categories today, and the rules are simple enough to keep in your head:

  • Outsourced services — anything whose NetSuite item type is a Service.
  • Primary packaging — SKUs beginning 0LAB, 0PACK, 0XFUC or 0XSAC.
  • Structural components — everything else.

The prefix list is a configuration setting, so the taxonomy can be extended without touching code.

Comparing two SKUs

Pick a second finished good and the Comparative panel appears: the COGS difference, the difference within each category, and a component-level diff. Components present in both at the same quantity and cost are hidden — what remains is ranked by euro impact, so the top of the table is always the reason the two products cost differently. A red delta means the first SKU costs more.

This is the intended use of the FG Reference List: load the former version of a SKU, put it side by side with the current one, and read off exactly which change moved the cost.

Downloads

CSV gives the indented bill of materials as shown. XLSX gives four sheets: BOM Detail, COGS Summary (including margin and coverage), Cost Source Audit (every leaf with its source and window) and, when a comparison is active, Comparison.

Field Reference

Available Today vs. Committable — What's the Difference?

Available Today is what you can ship right now, after accounting for all orders that are due today or are already late. It can be negative if there are more overdue orders than current stock.

Committable looks further out — it's the maximum buildable quantity over the next 120 days, minus all open demand (regardless of ship date). This is the ATP (Available-to-Promise) figure: how many additional units you can safely commit to new customers without jeopardising existing orders.

Example

A SKU shows Available Today = −12 (existing late orders exceed current stock), but Committable = 38. This means 12 late orders need attention, but after those are accounted for, there are 38 units of future capacity that can be safely promised to new orders.

The Planning Horizon (120 Days)

Only purchase orders with an expected receipt date within the next 120 days are included in the staircase calculation. POs beyond 120 days exist in NetSuite but are not shown. The horizon is configurable by an admin.

Overdue POs

Purchase orders whose expected receipt date has already passed are not counted as received. They are automatically rescheduled to today + 7 days in the calculation, appearing as an upcoming supply event. This provides a realistic (slightly conservative) picture.

How Availability Is Calculated

Every SKU is calculated twice — once for VERRAYES and once for USA-3PL — and each produces the same two headline numbers:

MetricColumnMeaning
ATS — Available to SellAvail TodayWhat you can promise and ship today that no already-booked order will reclaim later. It is the lowest point of Net Available across the whole staircase, not just today's stock.
ATP — Available to PromiseNext Avail / Next Qty / CommittableWhen and how much new supply becomes safely promisable in the future. Next Avail is the first future date where promisable units increase; Committable is the peak future capacity minus all open demand.

Both come from the same staircase: a time-phased list of every supply and demand event, where each step recomputes Buildable, Cumulative Demand and Net Available. What differs between the two locations is how supply gets onto the staircase.

VERRAYES logic (manufacturing)

  • Buildable = full multi-level BOM explosion: how many finished units the components on hand (plus components arriving on open POs / work orders by that date) can produce. The scarcest component sets the ceiling and is shown as the Top Constraint.
  • Supply events = component PO receipts and subcontract Work Order completions. Each event date gets a small manufacturing pad (+7 days); overdue POs are rescheduled to today + 7.
  • ATS = the minimum Net Available across the staircase (looks ahead so near-term booked orders can't be double-promised). Next Avail = the first future step where promisable units rise.

USA-3PL logic (the intercompany in-transit cascade)

ATS (Avail Today) is simple and unchanged: finished-goods on hand in the US warehouse, netted against US orders. Goods still travelling from Italy are not on hand, so they never inflate today's number.

ATP (the timing of incoming supply) is the smart part. The US is supplied by intercompany POs shipped from Italy, but the PO's expected receipt date is not trustworthy on its own — Italy may not have shipped yet even though the date is near. So each intercompany PO's open quantity is split into pieces and each piece is dated by physical reality:

BranchWhen it appliesDate used
1 · In transitUnits already shipped from Italy (confirmed by Italy's fulfilments) but not yet received in the US.Actual ship date + 28 days transit. If that has already passed, it falls to today + 7.
2 · Ship-now (Verrayes has stock)Not yet shipped, but VERRAYES has free stock to send. Bounded by VERRAYES Avail Today.The later of the PO receipt date or today + 28 days — because nothing has shipped, the soonest the US can receive is one transit away (the "transit floor").
3 · Wait for replenishmentNot yet shipped and VERRAYES has no free stock now (Committed / Incoming).VERRAYES Next Avail date + 28 days transit.
4 · No replenishmentVERRAYES is Out of Stock with no replenishment date.Does not land — contributes to US Out of Stock.
5 · No PO yet (planning signal)VERRAYES has stock but no intercompany PO has been raised.today + 60 days, for the shortfall — assumes a PO is triggered soon. (The only branch not backed by an existing order.)

Direct-vendor US POs (not intercompany) keep trusting their own PO date.

Worked example — why a date can be later than the PO says

A SKU has an intercompany PO for 10 units with a receipt date of 15 Jul, but Italy has shipped none of them, and VERRAYES has stock. Because nothing is in transit, the earliest the US could receive — if Italy shipped today — is one transit away. With today at 29 Jun, that floor is 27 Jul (today + 28). So Next Avail shows 27 Jul, not the optimistic 15 Jul. This is branch 2 (ship-now with the transit floor) in action.

Manual overrides feed the cascade

The US calculation reads VERRAYES's final numbers, so if a VERRAYES SKU is manually overridden, that override drives the US timing too. If VERRAYES is genuinely out of stock and not overridden, the not-yet-shipped US units will not appear as incoming.

Pads are admin-configurable

The 28-day transit, the 60-day no-PO lead time, and the 7-day buffers all live in config.json settings (usa3pl_intransit_pad_days, usa3pl_nopo_pad_days, overdue_po_buffer_days) and can be tuned without code changes.

Understanding the Data

Prices, ASP & Margin

Several parts of the dashboard show money rather than units: the Value €/$ toggle on the availability table, the revenue weighting behind the Intelligence signals, the committable value on the WBR cards, and the gross margin on the BOM tab. All of them are driven by one number per SKU per location — the average selling price, or ASP. This section explains exactly what goes into it, because the answer is less obvious than it looks.

One definition, everywhere

As of August 2026 there is a single price engine shared by every tab. Before that, the availability tabs used a definition that turned out to have several problems; those are described at the end of this section so that anyone comparing old screenshots to new ones knows why the numbers moved.

Two ways to measure a price

The engine computes both and prefers the first:

BasisWhat it measuresWeighted by
RealisedWhat customers actually paid — invoices and cash sales over the last twelve months.Units invoiced
Order bookWhat is currently agreed but not yet shipped — open sales order lines.Units still open

Realised is used wherever it exists. Where a SKU has had no sales in the last twelve months, the order book fills the gap. This cascade is deliberate: measured across the current catalogue, realised prices exist for roughly 268 of 318 SKUs in Italy and 150 in the USA, while the order book covers 310 and 249. Using realised alone would have lost coverage; using the order book alone would have measured aspiration rather than outcome.

What is included, and what is not

Every price is:

  • Net of discounts. The figure used is the line's value divided by its quantity, which is what remains after any partner, volume or promotional discount. This matters more than it sounds — realised wholesale prices run materially below list.
  • Net of VAT. Tax sits on its own line in NetSuite and never enters the calculation, on eCommerce orders or anywhere else.
  • In the right currency. Italian figures are euros and US figures are dollars, regardless of the currency the customer was actually billed in. Orders taken in Swiss francs, sterling, Canadian dollars or Czech koruna are converted by NetSuite before they reach the calculation.
  • Free of shipping and handling charges, which are separate lines and are excluded.

Which orders count

Not every transaction represents a commercial sale, so a list of order types is excluded from every price calculation, on every tab:

ExcludedWhy
Intercompany SellItaly selling to the US subsidiary at transfer price. Not a market price.
GiftSamples and giveaways. These carry a list price on the line but zero actual value.
Product Athlete, ExpertVoice OrderSponsorship and pro-deal channels.
Loan for use, Repair, Warranty Repair, Replacement OrderService movements, not sales.
R&D and CertificationsInternal consumption.

Everything else counts, including all the direct-to-consumer channels. Shopify, Amazon, Private Sport Shop and Outdoor Pro Link sell at retail prices well above wholesale, and they are deliberately blended in, weighted by volume. The ASP is therefore a true realised average across the channel mix, not a wholesale price list. A SKU with heavy eCommerce demand will show a higher ASP than one sold only through distributors, and that is the intended behaviour.

The excluded list is a configuration setting, so it can be changed without code. The same list governs the S&OP tab, so demand values there are consistent with availability values.

How margin is calculated on the BOM tab

The BOM tab is stricter than the rest of the dashboard, for two reasons.

First, it uses realised prices only, with no order-book fallback. A margin quoted on cost of goods has to be measured against revenue actually earned; falling back to an order book would mix a real cost against an aspiration. Where a product has had no sales in the window, the margin simply reads as a dash rather than being estimated.

Second, it uses the Italian euro price only. The COGS on that tab is a euro procurement cost incurred at Verrayes; pairing it with a US landed selling price would compare two things that do not belong together. Read the BOM margin as an Italy wholesale-and-direct blended margin at factory cost.

The margin is not a P&L margin

It is the realised ASP less the bill-of-materials cost. It carries none of the exclusions listed under the BOM tab — no internal labour, overhead, freight, duties or scrap — so it will read higher than any accounting gross margin. Use it to compare products against each other, not to report profitability.

What changed in August 2026

Prices in the availability and Intelligence tabs moved when this rework shipped. Four defects were fixed at once, all of them pushing in the same direction — the old figures were too high:

  • Discounts were invisible. The old calculation read the pre-discount unit price. NetSuite applies the discount to the line's value and repeats it on a separate line, so the price field never reflected it. On one crampon, the realised price was about seventeen per cent below the figure the dashboard was showing.
  • Currencies were mixed. Orders placed in foreign currencies contributed their raw foreign amounts to a figure labelled euros. A Czech order was contributing a number roughly twenty-four times too large.
  • Intercompany transfers counted as sales. Italy's transfer price to the US subsidiary was averaged into the Italian ASP across more than two hundred SKUs.
  • Free samples counted at list price. Several hundred US sample lines carried a full retail price with zero value attached, inflating the USA-3PL figures.

If you are comparing a current figure against a screenshot from before August 2026, expect the new number to be lower, and to be more correct.

When to Refresh Data

The dashboard data is static until manually refreshed. You should run a refresh:

  • Weekly — at minimum, once a week on a fixed day (e.g. Monday morning).
  • Before key sales calls or forecasting meetings — to ensure figures are current.

FG Reference List

A reference SKU is a former version of a product still on the FG list. Loading one lets the BOM tab cost it and put it side by side with the current version, which is the quickest way to see what a design or sourcing change did to the cost.

BOM tab only

Reference SKUs are exploded and costed for the BOM tab and appear in both of its dropdowns, tagged reference. They do not enter the availability calculation, the Manufacturing tab or S&OP — those remain driven by the FG list alone.

Exclusion List

Items on the exclusion list are treated as having unlimited supply — they are excluded from BOM constraint calculations. Use this for items that are purchased from external suppliers on a non-constrained basis (e.g. retail packaging, labels).

CSV format: internal_id, item_name — one row per item, no header.

# Example exclusion list CSV

7900, 0PACK.ENV
Note

The exclusion list applies globally — excluded items are unconstrained for all finished goods. There is no per-FG exclusion at this time.

Embargo List

Embargoed SKUs have their availability suppressed to zero until the specified date. Use this for products in final production that should not yet appear as available to sell.

CSV format: internal_id, item_name, embargo_date — one row per item, no header. Date format: YYYY-MM-DD.

# Example embargo list CSV
8001, PIATE+.NEW24, 2026-09-01
8002, TRIGRIP.24.S, 2026-08-15
USA-3PL embargo pad

Embargo dates at USA-3PL are automatically extended by 90 days to account for Italy-to-USA supply chain lead time. So an embargo of 2026-09-01 will show as unavailable at USA-3PL until approximately 2026-12-01. No action is needed — this happens automatically.

Overrides

An override lets you set a finished good's availability manually, bypassing all calculation for that SKU at one location. Use it when you know the real availability (e.g. a confirmed allocation or a correction) and want the dashboard to show it regardless of what the engine computes.

CSV format: no header, 5 columns — Internal ID, Location, Overridden Today Avail, Overridden Next Avail, Overridden Next Qty. Location is Verrayes or USA - 3PL. Overridden Next Avail is a date (YYYY-MM-DD).

# Example overrides CSV (no header)
1116, Verrayes, 50, 2026-07-01, 100
7420, USA - 3PL, 0, ,

What an override does on the affected SKU:

  • Avail Today, Next Avail, Next Qty take the values from the CSV.
  • Status is derived: Today > 0 → In Stock; otherwise a Next Avail date present → Incoming; otherwise Out of Stock.
  • Max Build, Committable, Top Constraint and the expandable staircase are blanked (shown as —) — the calculation is bypassed.
  • Open Demand is kept from live data; it is not overridden.
  • Any embargo on that SKU is ignored while the override is in place.
Blank means empty

If you load a SKU into the Overrides list, declare both the Today and the Next values. A blank cell is overridden to empty — it is not back-filled from the calculation. In the example above, 7420 is forced to Out of Stock with no Today and no Next value.

Overridden SKUs are flagged in the table with a ✎ icon and an amber left edge, so they are easy to spot. They are excluded from the Intelligence view and the Committable Distribution chart (their computed inputs are intentionally blank).

Reference

Glossary

ATP
Available to Promise. The number of units that can be safely committed to new orders without jeopardising existing open orders. Shown as the "Committable" column.
ASP / Selling Price
Average selling price per unit, computed as the quantity-weighted average of open sales-order line rates (EUR for Verrayes, USD for USA-3PL). Drives the Units/Value €$ toggle. A SKU with no priced open orders has no ASP, so its value cells are left blank.
BOM
Bill of Materials. The list of sub-components and raw materials required to produce one unit of a finished good. The dashboard explodes BOMs recursively to compute true production capacity.
Buildable
The maximum units that could theoretically be produced at a given date, given current inventory plus any POs received up to that date. Does not subtract demand.
Committable
Max buildable at the 120-day planning horizon, minus all open demand. The safe ATP figure for new orders.
Embargo
An administrative hold on a SKU. Availability is suppressed to zero until the embargo date is reached.
Override
A manual setting (admin-managed) that replaces an FG's computed availability at one location with values entered by hand, bypassing the calculation. Overridden SKUs are flagged with a ✎ icon; Open Demand stays live, other computed fields are blanked.
FG / Finished Good
A product that is sold to customers (as opposed to a raw material or sub-assembly used in production).
Intercompany PO
A purchase order raised by Grivel Corp. (USA) against Grivel S.r.l. (Italy) to transfer stock between entities. These appear as supply at USA-3PL.
Lifecycle
The product life stage: Active, New Product, Discontinued, or End Of Life. Pulled from NetSuite.
Net Available
Buildable minus cumulative demand at a given date. What is shown in the Avail Today column and in each staircase step.
Open Demand
The total quantity of unfulfilled sales order lines for a SKU, across all ship dates (past and future).
Planning Horizon
The time window (default 120 days) within which PO receipts are included in staircase calculations.
Staircase
The time-phased availability view showing how buildable, demand, and net available change at each future supply or demand event.
Top Constraint
The BOM component (raw material or sub-assembly) whose scarcity most limits production of a finished good.
VERRAYES
Grivel's manufacturing facility in Verrayes, Aosta Valley, Italy. Supply Chain Control Tower shows full BOM-aware availability for this location.

Frequently Asked Questions

The data looks outdated. What do I do?

Check the "Data as of…" timestamp in the header. If it's more than a week old, contact the person responsible for data refreshes (currently Davide Pezzia / Operations). Refreshes are manual and require running the Python script on the local machine with NetSuite credentials.

A SKU I know exists is not showing up.

The dashboard only shows SKUs that appear on the Finished Goods list managed by an admin. The SKU may exist in NetSuite but not yet be on the FG list. Contact an admin to add it.

Avail Today is negative. Is that a data error?

No. A negative Avail Today means open orders whose expected ship date is today or earlier exceed the current buildable supply. These are essentially overdue or unfillable orders from a supply perspective.

Why is USA-3PL Out of Stock when VERRAYES shows In Stock?

The two locations are independent. Being In Stock at VERRAYES means the product is available in Italy. For it to be available at USA-3PL, an intercompany purchase order must have been placed and the stock physically transferred. Check the staircase view at USA-3PL to see if any intercompany supply is scheduled.

What does "No PO" mean in the Top Constraint column?

The constraining component has no open purchase order currently in NetSuite. This means the constraint will not be resolved until a PO is raised. Escalate to procurement immediately if the SKU has significant open demand.

Can I request a specific SKU be embargoed?

Yes. Provide the NetSuite internal ID, item name, and desired embargo date to an admin, who will add it to the embargo list and trigger a refresh.

The AI assistant says it can't answer a question about a specific SKU.

The AI receives a summary of the top items (by demand, constraint, and committable), not the full 332-item dataset. For individual SKU details, use the search field in the availability table and expand the row to see the full staircase.
